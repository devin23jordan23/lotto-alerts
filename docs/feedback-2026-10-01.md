# Lotto feedback: two failed puts, the IWM call, and missed October 1 moves

All times are New York time. Evidence is the worker's saved Schwab option-chain snapshots, saved decisions, and Schwab one-minute regular-session bars. Option returns below compare the *sampled ask at the stated time* with the *highest later sampled bid*; they are opportunities observed in the data, not actual executions. A peak can occur between one-minute samples, and an alert can arrive after the quote timestamp. Intraday option volume is cumulative and does not show who bought, sold, opened, or closed. The scanner did not save implied volatility, theta, or vega for these sessions, so an IV or dealer-hedging explanation cannot be established from this evidence.

This trade peaked quickly and did **not** persist into the close. Its success should be learned as a fast 0DTE pop, separate from the goal of finding names that close near session highs.

The raw score ranked the failed META put (80.56) above the winning IWM call (72.33). Raising the global score threshold would have removed the winner first. Price-to-trigger distance, freshness of the directional break, recent opposing flow, and time to option peak carry information that the current single score does not represent well. The three alerts are far too small a sample for a new hard threshold.

## SPY and QQQ: synchronized reversal missed by the score gate

The selloff low in both SPY and QQQ occurred around 11:11. SPY reclaimed intraday VWAP around 13:04; QQQ reclaimed it around 13:11. IWM had already been holding above VWAP. By 13:20, SPY had a price continuation setup, QQQ had a reversal setup, and all three were advancing. SPY and QQQ both showed call-led three-strike flow during 13:18–13:26. This is a plausible *joint* precursor, visible before the sharp 13:30 extension, not proof that calls caused the stock move.

At **13:23**, SPY's saved decision selected the Oct 2 $767C: score **65.13**, with **“score below threshold” as its only blocker**. The stock had a continuation setup, 2.13× five-minute local RVOL, 3.21× option acceleration, a 12-strike call cluster, and supportive QQQ context. The same-day $765C was quoted $0.45/$0.46 with delta 0.287, gamma 0.121, a 2.2% spread, and 18,109 additional contracts since 13:18. Its best later sampled bid through 14:05 was **$1.15 at 13:47**, a hypothetical **+150%** from $0.46. SPY's Oct 1 $764C was $0.54 ask at 13:20 and later reached $1.77 bid (+228%). At 13:18, the $765C was $0.21 ask, but SPY's saved decision had **no developing price setup** yet. The 13:23 case is the stronger no-lookahead miss.

At **13:23**, QQQ selected the Oct 2 $745C with score **60.59**, also blocked only by the score floor. Its same-day $744C was $0.47/$0.48, delta 0.238, gamma 0.091, and gained 7,276 cumulative contracts since 13:18. The best later sampled bid was **$1.54 at 13:47** (+221% from $0.48). Its $0.19 ask at 13:18 could imply a larger hindsight return, but delta 0.115 was below the scanner's 0.12 minimum then. Neither index produced a lotto alert.

The existing single-name score understates a *coordinated index reversal*: SPY and QQQ both had valid price structure, local volume, and call breadth by 13:23, while IWM was coiled near its own breakout. The score's spot-adjusted strike-migration component was **zero** for SPY and QQQ. The current algorithm also picks the highest scoring contract across 0–1DTE; it selected next-day calls even though liquid same-day calls existed. A separate, prospective index-reversal hypothesis should evaluate simultaneous stock/option evidence and rank contracts by expected move, spread, delta/gamma, and time remaining. Simply lowering 72 globally would also admit many failed setups.

## Feedback to test, not silently promote

1. Record the earliest qualifying timestamp, contract ask, delivered alert ask, sampled peak bid and its time, 5/15/30/60-minute bid returns, close bid, trigger distance in prior ATR units, and the stock's eventual close location. Future nightly reports now calculate these **alert feedback** fields. Keep both “fast pop” and “end-of-day runner” labels.
2. Shadow-test a **coordinated index-reversal** candidate when SPY and QQQ both reclaim/hold VWAP, show recent call clusters and local-volume expansion, and have liquid 0–1DTE contracts near a usable strike. Compare its opportunity and false-positive rates with similar prior sessions. Do not substitute a blanket score cut.
3. Shadow-test **entry extension** and **trigger proximity** for opening breaks and coiled setups. The winning IWM call was 0.017 ATR from trigger; the failed META put was 0.084 ATR away; the failed IWM put had already overshot by 0.47 ATR. These three examples suggest a useful feature, not a fitted production cutoff.
4. Study **earlier, clearly labeled watches** for first-qualified high-conviction cases. The IWM call's 2-minute confirmation cost 28.6% in ask premium. Preserve the confirmed alert path until earlier watches are evaluated against failed first qualifications.
5. Study separate **opening-drive** and **price-led/event** lanes for MRNA, AAOI, and MU. Option spread, expiry, moneyness, and event-related premium must be assessed alongside price. A fixed $2.50 cap cannot represent the same risk in IWM and MU. Starting with the next scan, raw chain implied volatility, theta, and vega will be saved when Schwab supplies them; historical values for these examples are unavailable.

None of these observations establishes dealer positioning or a gamma squeeze. Contract gamma describes sensitivity of an option's delta; it does not disclose the market maker's net position. [Cboe's 0DTE analysis](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options/) explains why gross volume alone is insufficient to infer market-wide hedging pressure.
