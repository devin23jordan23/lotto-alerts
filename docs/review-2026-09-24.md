# Why Sep 24 produced zero lotto ideas

Read-only audit of the production lotto SQLite database, emitted at worker
startup on Sep 25. No historical options tape or unrecorded quotes were inferred.

- The worker recorded 39,737 stock discovery observations across all 102 names.
- It saved 4,679 option snapshots across just 33 names because chain collection
  was limited to 12 simultaneous names. A stock outside those 33 could not have
  generated an options-confirmed idea.
- 3,491 decisions had no evaluable options candidate. This count includes
  missing observation windows, unavailable liquid contracts and missing active
  adjacent strikes; the previous log reason merged these cases.
- 284 decisions lacked a same-time historical volume baseline and 213 had an
  incomplete one-minute bar path. Those names were blocked for data quality.
- The best scored price/option candidate reached 80.4 (ORCL calls); several
  INTC call coils scored about 78–80 with no blockers at 2:28, 2:29 and
  2:30 p.m. ET. No recommendation was queued because the live confirmation
  required **three elapsed minutes after the first qualifying observation**,
  which means a fourth one-minute observation. Three consecutive bars were
  insufficient. This was an implementation mismatch with the intended
  three-observation confirmation.
- An otherwise strong USO put reversal scored 78.45 but was vetoed by a
  weak unrelated benchmark. A broad sector veto can hide stock-specific moves.

Changes for Sep 25: require three consecutive one-minute observations (two
elapsed minutes), allow strong stock leadership against a weak independent
benchmark, and expand the options pool to 18 names with four rotating
exploration slots. The score cutoff, current quote freshness, contract spread,
delta, strike activity and daily alert limits remain intact. The rotation
improves coverage but cannot guarantee every one of 102 names receives an
options chain during one session.

The saved decisions support these missed-candidate examples. They do not prove
that any option would have returned 500%, and a candidate can still fail after
an alert. Subsequent sessions need to measure both useful ideas and false starts.
